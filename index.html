Floor.top Payout Model

Source: clickihood.com/whitepapers
Read 2026-09-20 · Robinhood Chain · Sept 2026 filing

How fees actually work: the launchpad's 1% trading fee never pays the leaderboard or NFT holders directly. Every launch instead carves off 1–10% of that coin's fixed 1B supply as a free community allocation (founder's choice, per launch), split 50/50 between all 9,999 employee NFTs and the top-100 NUTS leaderboard. The trading fee's 0.45–0.9% vault cut only touches you indirectly: it buys stock that backs every coin in circulation, setting a floor price under the free tokens you already received. So "value of the fees" really means: backing-per-coin (driven by fees) × tokens you were allocated (driven by supply %, not fees).

Pot 1 · Fixed

Employee NFT pool

50% of the community allocation, split into one equal claim per NFT across all 9,999 — whoever holds it during that launch's 7-day claim window. Doesn't matter how well the NFT is earning NUTS; only that you hold it.

Pot 2 · Skill-weighted

Leaderboard pool

The other 50%, split among the top 100 lifetime-NUTS earners, in proportion to lifetime NUTS — not evenly. Whitepaper gives no distribution curve, so this model lets you set how top-heavy it is.

What the whitepapers don't tell you

The gaps that most affect this valuation, ranked by how much they swing the answer.
01
Founder's allocation % is unconstrained (1–10%, a 10× swing)
No stated default, no incentive mechanism disclosed for why a founder would pick 10% over 1%. This single knob moves every dollar figure in this model by 10×, and it's set per-launch by someone with no disclosed obligation to the game's community.
02
No launch cadence or platform-adoption target
Total value compounds with the number of coins that actually graduate (reach 4.2069 ETH raised) — there's no roadmap figure for launches/month, so a per-launch payout is where this model stops; scaling that to a longer horizon is your own assumption, not the whitepaper's.
03
Backing-per-coin is a floor, not the expected price
The market-cap slider and table above let you separate the two explicitly: floor value is mechanically guaranteed by the vault, market value is pure speculation the whitepaper offers zero guidance on. Toggle "Value basis" to compare them directly for your own scenario.
04
Redemption behavior is illustrative, not guaranteed
The whitepaper's own Month-1/3/6 numbers assume specific redemption rates (0%, 7%, 15% of supply) that shrink the denominator and inflate backing-per-coin. Nothing forces holders to actually redeem at that pace; the "0% conservative" option in this model is the safer default.
05
One NFT/rank claim per graduated launch, not a standing royalty
This is stated, but easy to miss: you must be holding the NFT (or still be top-100) at each individual launch's 7-day claim window. Selling an NFT forfeits all future launch claims on it to the buyer — there's no accrual to a wallet independent of holding.
